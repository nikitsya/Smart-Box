# Temperature Research Sources

## Temperature inside parked vehicles

**Dadour et al. – Temperature variations in a parked vehicle**

Research found that the temperature inside a parked vehicle can rise more than 20°C above the outside temperature.

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21067876/

## Drug storage temperatures in emergency vehicles

**Environmental temperature stress on drugs in prehospital EMS**

The study measured drug storage temperatures in emergency vehicles and found that recommended storage temperatures were
exceeded for significant periods of time.

Source:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12694141/

## Controlled drugs

According to the PHECC Clinical Practice Guidelines (Advanced Paramedic) and the Misuse of Drugs Regulations (Schedule
2 & 3), compulsory safe storage in the Controlled Drug Safe applies specifically to Morphine, Fentanyl, Midazolam, and
Ketamine out of the entire range of ambulance medications.

| Medicine          | Amount per ampoule or vial |
|-------------------|----------------------------|
| Morphine sulphate | 10 mg / 1 ml               |
| Fentanyl          | 100 micrograms / 2 ml      |
| Ketamine          | 200 mg / 20 ml             |
| Ketamine          | 500 mg / 5 ml              |

Source: https://www.medicalcouncil.ie/news-and-publications/publications/safe-prescribing-and-dispensing-of-controlled-drugs-2024.pdf.

## Low-temperature risk and prototype alert threshold

NHS Specialist Pharmacy Service guidance identifies exposure below 0°C as a freezing risk for most medicines, with
potential
irreversible damage. Required storage conditions must also be maintained during transport.

TempSafe uses a project-defined low-temperature alert at **≤ 0°C** to flag potential freezing risk. No separate
low-temperature warning stage is included in the prototype.

This is an alert threshold, not a verified minimum storage temperature for each medicine. It does not establish 0–25°C
as a
universally safe storage range or determine whether a medicine remains suitable for use.

Source: [NHS SPS — Temperature control whilst transporting medicines](https://sps.nhs.uk/articles/temperature-control-whilst-transporting-medicines/).

## Relevance to TempSafe

These studies support the need for continuous temperature monitoring of controlled drugs stored in emergency vehicles.
